**The right GPS tracking system for a fleet in Bharat is the one that keeps reporting on your actual routes, meets the certification your vehicles need, gives your team answers in the way they already work, and protects your data.** Features on a brochure look similar across providers. These checks show the real differences.

## 1. Device quality and positioning

- **Multi-constellation positioning.** Devices that use GPS together with NavIC (Bharat's own satellite system), GLONASS and Galileo hold a more accurate position in cities and hilly areas.
- **Store and forward.** On highways and in rural areas the mobile network drops out. A good device stores positions while offline and uploads them later, so trip history has no gaps.
- **Backup battery and tamper alerts.** You should be told if someone cuts the device's power or removes it.
- **Dual-SIM or multi-network connectivity** for vehicles that run through areas with patchy coverage.

## 2. Certification and regulation

- **AIS-140.** Public service vehicles, and some commercial and school vehicles depending on the state, need an AIS-140 compliant tracking device and emergency buttons. Ask for the device's certificate and the state approvals that apply to your registration.
- **Data protection.** Bharat's Digital Personal Data Protection Act applies to driver data such as names, phone numbers and location linked to a person. Ask where data is stored, who can see it and how long it is kept.

## 3. How your team will actually use it

This is where most tracking projects succeed or fail. A dashboard nobody opens does not save fuel.

- **WhatsApp and phone access.** Can an owner or supervisor ask "Where is my vehicle?" on WhatsApp, or by phone, and get an answer without logging in?
- **Regional languages.** Can drivers and supervisors use it in Hindi, Telugu, Tamil, Kannada, Malayalam, Marathi, Bengali or their own language?
- **Alerts that reach someone.** An app notification at 2 a.m. is easy to miss. Look for alert phone calls that escalate to the next person if nobody responds.
- **Mobile apps** for managers and a separate app for drivers.

## 4. Sharing with customers

Shippers increasingly ask transporters for live location. Check whether you can share a tracking link for a single trip, and whether the provider can connect to shippers' systems without exposing your whole fleet. A neutral GPS data hub lets a shipper see only the trucks on its own trips, only during each trip, with your consent.

## 5. Features that pay for themselves

| Feature | What it saves |
|---|---|
| Fuel level sensor and drain alerts | Fuel theft and short-filling |
| Idling and speeding reports | Fuel and wear |
| Engine diagnostics and service reminders | Breakdowns and downtime |
| AI dash cams | Accidents, false claims and insurance cost |
| Trip and route reports | Detention time and disputes with customers |

You do not need everything on day one. Start with tracking, fuel and alerts, then add the rest.

## 6. Total cost, not just the device price

Ask every provider for an itemised quote covering:

- Device and installation, per vehicle
- Monthly or yearly subscription, per vehicle
- Sensors, cameras and SIM or data charges
- Support, training and replacement terms
- Contract length and exit terms, including whether you can export your data

## 7. Questions to ask before you sign

1. Can I run a pilot on my own vehicles first?
2. Which device models do you support, and can I keep mine?
3. Who owns the data, and can I export all of it at any time?
4. How do drivers and owners who do not use dashboards get information?
5. What happens if a device fails on the road?
