**Preventive maintenance services a vehicle on a fixed schedule. Predictive maintenance services it when data shows a component is about to fail.** Both aim to avoid breakdowns. They differ in how they decide when to act.

## Three approaches compared

| | Reactive | Preventive | Predictive |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Something breaks | Time, distance or engine hours | Data indicates wear or anomaly |
| Planning | None | Scheduled | Scheduled just in time |
| Downtime | Unplanned, often long | Planned, sometimes unnecessary | Planned and minimal |
| Cost profile | High repair and disruption cost | Moderate, some over-servicing | Lower, needs data investment |
| Data needed | None | Odometer or hours | Continuous vehicle data |

## Reactive maintenance

Fix it when it fails. It needs no planning but leads to roadside breakdowns, missed deliveries and secondary damage. It is acceptable only for low-cost, non-critical items.

## Preventive maintenance

Service at set intervals, for example every fixed number of kilometres or engine hours. It is simple and effective, and it is the foundation of any maintenance programme. Its weakness is that it treats every vehicle the same, regardless of how hard it has worked.

Telematics improves preventive maintenance by reading the real odometer and engine hours automatically, so reminders are always accurate.

## Predictive maintenance

Use live data to detect early signs of failure. Examples:

- **Battery.** Cranking voltage that falls a little each week predicts a no-start.
- **Cooling system.** Coolant temperature creeping above the vehicle's normal range suggests a failing thermostat, fan or leak.
- **Fault codes.** A pattern of intermittent codes often precedes a hard failure.
- **Fuel efficiency.** An unexplained drop can indicate injector or filter problems.

AI models learn what is normal for each vehicle and flag deviations, giving the workshop days of notice.

## How to move towards predictive

1. Get the basics right: accurate, usage-based preventive schedules.
2. Connect vehicles so fault codes and engine data flow automatically.
3. Act on fault-code alerts with clear severity levels.
4. Add predictive models for high-impact components.
5. Feed workshop findings back, so predictions improve.

## The practical answer

Use preventive maintenance as the baseline and add predictive alerts where the data supports it. The combination delivers the fewest surprises at the lowest cost.
