**Telematics is the technology that collects data from vehicles and assets and sends it to software where people can act on it.** The word combines *telecommunications* and *informatics*. In a fleet, it means knowing where every vehicle is, how it is being driven and what condition it is in, without having to call the driver.

## How telematics works

Every telematics system has the same four parts.

1. **A device in the vehicle.** A small unit with a satellite-positioning receiver, motion sensors and a mobile-network modem. It connects to vehicle power and, usually, to the engine's data port.
2. **A network connection.** The device sends data over the mobile network every few seconds. If there is no coverage, it stores the data and sends it later.
3. **A cloud platform.** Servers receive, process and store the data, and apply rules and AI to detect events worth knowing about.
4. **Apps for people.** Managers see maps, alerts and reports on the web and on their phones. Drivers often have their own app.

## What data does telematics collect?

| Data | Where it comes from | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Location, speed, heading | Satellite positioning (GPS and other GNSS) | Where vehicles are and where they have been |
| Harsh braking, acceleration, cornering | Accelerometer | How safely vehicles are driven |
| Fuel level and consumption | Engine data or tank sensor | Efficiency, refills and theft |
| Fault codes, engine hours, odometer | Vehicle CAN bus or OBD-II port | Vehicle health and service needs |
| Video and AI events | Dash cams | Risky behaviour and incident evidence |
| Temperature, doors, load | Add-on sensors | Cargo condition and security |

## What do fleets use telematics for?

- **Visibility.** Dispatch the nearest vehicle, answer customer questions instantly and verify that work was done.
- **Cost control.** Reduce idling, detours, unauthorised use and fuel theft. Service vehicles on time.
- **Safety.** Identify risky driving, coach drivers and keep video evidence of incidents.
- **Compliance.** Keep automatic records for regulators and customers.
- **Planning.** Use real utilisation data to decide how many vehicles you really need.

## What has changed: the role of AI

Early telematics produced reports, and someone had to read them. Modern platforms use AI to do the reading. Vision models spot a distracted driver in real time. Predictive models notice a battery failing before it strands a vehicle. Anomaly detection flags a fuel drain at 2 a.m. The manager's job shifts from searching for problems to acting on a short, prioritised list.

## How to get started

Start with a clear goal, such as cutting fuel cost or improving on-time delivery. Choose devices that suit each vehicle type, involve drivers early and explain how the data will be used. Then review results monthly and expand from there.
