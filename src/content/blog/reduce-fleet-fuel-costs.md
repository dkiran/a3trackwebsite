**Fuel is typically the largest variable cost of running a fleet, and a meaningful share of it is wasted through idling, poor driving habits, inefficient routes, poor maintenance and theft.** The good news is that every one of these can be measured and reduced.

## Driving behaviour

**1. Cut idling.** An idling engine burns fuel and goes nowhere. Set an idling limit, alert drivers in the cab and review the worst offenders weekly.

**2. Control speed.** Fuel consumption rises sharply at higher speeds. Enforce sensible limits and monitor speeding against posted road limits.

**3. Smooth out harsh driving.** Hard acceleration and late braking waste fuel and wear out brakes and tyres. Driver scorecards make the pattern visible.

**4. Coach and reward.** Share scores with drivers, coach the few who need it and recognise the best. Incentives tied to efficiency work.

## Routing and utilisation

**5. Optimise routes.** Route-planning software reduces distance and time, especially for multi-stop work.

**6. Avoid empty running.** Use live visibility to find return loads and combine trips.

**7. Right-size the fleet.** Utilisation reports show which vehicles are under-used. Fewer, busier vehicles cost less.

## Vehicle condition

**8. Keep tyres at the right pressure.** Under-inflated tyres increase rolling resistance and fuel use.

**9. Service on time.** Dirty filters, old oil and faulty injectors reduce efficiency. Use usage-based service reminders.

**10. Act on fault codes.** Many engine faults increase fuel consumption long before they cause a breakdown.

## Fuel security

**11. Monitor the tank.** A fuel-level sensor shows every refill and every drain, with time and location. Sudden drops while parked are flagged instantly.

**12. Reconcile purchases.** Compare fuel bills and fuel-card transactions with what actually entered the tank. Mismatches reveal short-filling and fraud.

## Putting it together

Do not try all twelve at once. A practical sequence is:

1. Install tracking and establish a baseline for fuel efficiency by vehicle.
2. Tackle idling and speeding, which are easy to measure.
3. Add fuel-level monitoring on high-consumption vehicles.
4. Introduce driver scorecards and incentives.
5. Review routes and fleet size with the data you now have.
