**An effective driver safety programme combines a clear policy, fair measurement, timely coaching and visible recognition.** Technology supplies the data. Culture decides whether it changes anything.

## 1. Set a clear policy

Write a short, plain policy covering speed, seat belts, mobile phones, fatigue, alcohol and vehicle checks. Explain why each rule exists. Have every driver read and acknowledge it.

## 2. Measure fairly

Use telematics to measure the behaviours most linked to collisions:

- Speeding, especially relative to the posted limit
- Harsh braking, acceleration and cornering
- Distraction and drowsiness, where AI cameras are fitted
- Seat belt use
- Driving hours and rest

Normalise by distance so that drivers who cover more kilometres are not penalised. Share the scoring method openly.

## 3. Alert in the moment

In-cab alerts let drivers correct themselves immediately. Many risky events never need to reach a manager, which builds trust.

## 4. Coach, do not punish

Good coaching is specific, timely and respectful.

1. Review the event together, with the clip if there is one.
2. Ask the driver what happened before offering your view.
3. Agree one concrete change.
4. Follow up and acknowledge improvement.

## 5. Recognise good driving

Publish leaderboards, celebrate milestones and reward sustained improvement, not only top scores. Recognition from peers and managers is often worth more than money.

## 6. Look after vehicles and schedules

Safe driving needs safe vehicles and realistic schedules. Daily inspections, on-time maintenance and routes that do not demand speeding are part of the programme.

## 7. Review and improve

Track a small set of indicators monthly: events per 1,000 km, collisions, coaching completed and score trends. Adjust thresholds as the fleet improves.

## Getting driver buy-in

- Announce the programme before devices are installed.
- Emphasise protection and exoneration.
- Give drivers access to their own data.
- Apply the rules consistently, including to managers.
- Protect privacy outside working hours.

## The role of technology

Telematics and AI video make the programme measurable and scalable. They show where risk is concentrated, automate alerts and document progress for customers and insurers. They do not replace leadership.
