**AIS-140 is an Bharatiya automotive standard that sets requirements for vehicle location tracking devices (VLTDs) and emergency buttons in public service and certain commercial vehicles.** Its purpose is passenger safety and better oversight of public transport.

> This article is a general overview, not legal advice. Rules and enforcement vary by state and change over time. Always confirm current requirements with your state transport department.

## What does AIS-140 cover?

The standard describes what a compliant tracking device must do and how it must be tested. In broad terms:

- **Location tracking.** The device must determine position using satellite navigation, with support for Bharat's NavIC system as specified in the standard.
- **Emergency buttons.** Vehicles must have buttons that passengers or crew can press to raise an alert.
- **Communication with government systems.** The device must be able to send location and emergency data to designated back-end servers.
- **Dual connectivity.** An embedded SIM that can use more than one mobile network, to improve coverage.
- **Backup power.** An internal battery so the device keeps working if vehicle power is cut.
- **Tamper and health alerts.** Notifications for disconnection and device faults.
- **Data storage.** The ability to store data when the network is unavailable and send it later.

## Which vehicles does it apply to?

The requirement has been notified for public service vehicles, such as buses and taxis, and has been extended by various notifications and states to other categories, including some goods and school vehicles. Coverage and timelines differ by state, so check the rules that apply where your vehicles are registered.

## What makes a device compliant?

A device must be tested and certified by an authorised testing agency, and the device model must be approved for use in the relevant state. A valid fitment is usually recorded against the vehicle in the government vehicle database.

## How to prepare your fleet

1. **List affected vehicles** by category and state of registration.
2. **Choose certified devices** from a supplier who can show current certification and state approvals.
3. **Plan installation** to minimise vehicle downtime, including emergency button fitment and testing.
4. **Complete registration** of each fitment with the transport authority.
5. **Use the data.** A compliant device can do far more than satisfy a rule.
